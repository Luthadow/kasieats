## 1. COMPANY IDENTITY

### Legal Entity
- **Company Name:** KasiEats (Pty) Ltd
- **Registration Number:** To be registered in South Africa
- **Jurisdiction:** South Africa (Gauteng initially)
- **Business Model:** Multi-sided marketplace platform
- **Status:** Pre-revenue (MVP launch Week 12)

### Tagline
**"Bringing Township Flavour Home"**

### The Problem We Solve
Township food vendors are **invisible and unreachable** to customers who want authentic, local meals.

- Vendors rely on WhatsApp, Facebook, word-of-mouth
- Customers struggle to discover vendors, compare, order, pay
- No delivery infrastructure for informal food economy
- Vendors have no digital tools to manage business

### The Solution
**KasiEats** is the digital nervous system connecting:
- **Customers** (discovery → ordering → tracking)
- **Vendors** (order management → analytics → revenue)
- **Drivers** (delivery jobs → earnings → reputation)
- **Admin** (platform control → fraud detection → growth)

All orchestrated by **one central API brain**.

### Vision (Where We're Going)

**KasiEats becomes South Africa's largest digital marketplace for township-based small businesses.**

**Year 1:** Food only (Rustenburg → Gauteng)  
**Year 2-6:** Super-app expansion (produce, spaza, pharmacy, laundry, parcels)

## 2. VISION & MISSION

### Vision Statement

"To digitise the township economy and unlock billions of Rands in economic value for local entrepreneurs, creating the largest super-app for township commerce in Southern Africa."

### Mission Statement

"KasiEats empowers township food vendors to reach customers, enables customers to discover authentic local food, and creates economic opportunities for delivery drivers—all through one trusted platform."

- **Principle:** Drivers set their own hours, earn competitive rates
- **Action:** Fair commission (60-70% of delivery fee), transparent earnings
- **Outcome:** 100+ active drivers earning R3,000+/week

#### Pillar 4: Platform Trust & Safety
- **Principle:** KasiEats earns trust through transparency and compliance
- **Action:** POPIA-compliant, fraud detection, vendor verification, KYC
- **Outcome:** <0.1% fraud rate, 99.9% platform uptime

## 8. REVENUE MODELS

### Revenue Stream 1: Commission on Orders

**Model:** Platform takes % of order value

**Structure:**
- **Commission Rate:** 15% of order subtotal
- **Example:** R100 order → R15 to KasiEats, R85 to vendor

**Calculation:**
- Customer places R100 order
- Vendor receives: R100 - R15 (commission) = R85
- KasiEats keeps: R15

**Why This Rate:**
- Industry standard: 15-30%
- Competitive: Uber Eats = 30%, Mr Delivery = 25%
- Sustainable: Covers infrastructure + team + growth
- Vendor-friendly: 85% to vendor (vs. Uber's 70%)

**Annual Revenue (Projected):**
- Month 1: R2k (10 orders × R100 avg × 15%)
- Month 3: R30k (600 orders × R100 avg × 15%)
- Month 12: R450k (2,000 orders/month × R100 avg × 15%)

---

### Revenue Stream 2: Delivery Fees

**Model:** Platform charges customer for delivery

**Structure:**
- **Distance-Based Fee:**
  - 0-2km: R15 flat
  - 2-5km: R25
  - 5-10km: R40
  - 10km+: R60

**Split:**
- Driver receives: 60% of fee
- KasiEats keeps: 40% of fee
- Example: R25 delivery fee → R15 to driver, R10 to KasiEats

**Why This Model:**
- Transparent to customers
- Incentivizes driver efficiency
- Covers logistics costs

**Annual Revenue (Projected):**
- Month 1: R500 (10 orders × R25 avg fee × 40%)
- Month 3: R12k (600 orders × R25 avg fee × 40%)
- Month 12: R120k (2,000 orders/month × R25 avg fee × 40%)

---

## 12. CULTURAL PRINCIPLES

### How We Work

**Principle 1: Move Fast, But Right**
- Ship weekly, not monthly
- Production-grade code, not prototype quality
- Compliance and security built in, not bolted on

**Principle 2: Transparency Above All**
- Decisions are documented and public (to team)
- KPIs visible to everyone
- Failures discussed openly
- Commission structure transparent to vendors

**Principle 3: Customer Obsession**
- Every team member spends 2 hours/week on customer support
- Founder and CTO use app weekly as real users
- Monthly customer interviews mandatory

**Principle 4: Data-Driven**
- Every decision backed by data
- Vanity metrics avoided
- Weekly KPI reviews
- A/B testing for major changes

**Principle 5: Respect for the Township Economy**
- Vendors are not suppliers; they're partners
- Drivers are not workers; they're entrepreneurs
- Prices reflect real value, not extraction
- Profit ≠ Success; impact = Success
